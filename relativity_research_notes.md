# Special Relativity research basis

The advanced module will use an event-and-frame model rather than treating relativity as a list of isolated formulas. The Lorentz factor is dimensionless, approaches one at low relative speed, and grows sharply as relative velocity approaches the speed of light. The module will distinguish proper time from dilated time and proper length from contracted length, with contraction explicitly limited to the direction of relative motion. [1]

The spacetime interaction will use an x–ct coordinate system. Light worldlines are plotted at 45° when both axes share length units; a stationary object has a vertical worldline, while any material object remains inside the light cone. The event interval classification will be treated with care: causally related events are timelike, events outside each other’s light cones are spacelike, and light-connected events are null. [2]

The coordinate transformation panel will use the standard one-dimensional Lorentz relations for a frame S′ moving at constant speed v in the positive x direction: x′ = γ(x − vt) and t′ = γ(t − vx/c²). These reduce to the Galilean form at small v/c, and provide the basis for the lesson’s reference-frame calculator. [2]

# Relativity Observatory — Design Direction

## Three stylistic approaches considered

### 1. Observatory Ledger
**Very Brief Intro:** A midnight research observatory with quiet instrument panels, star-map coordinates, and warm astronomical annotation. It makes advanced theory feel measurable rather than mystical.

**Probability:** 0.06

### 2. Constructivist Spacetime
**Very Brief Intro:** A spare, high-contrast teaching system inspired by early modernist scientific posters, using angled axes and red event markers to make frames of reference physically legible.

**Probability:** 0.03

### 3. Photon Archive
**Very Brief Intro:** An archival document room where light cones, worldlines, and formal derivations are organised as annotated evidence. It is restrained, tactile, and academically serious.

**Probability:** 0.09

---

## Chosen approach: Observatory Ledger

### Design Movement
**Contemporary astronomical instrument design** fused with data-rich editorial layouts and precision observatory logs. The visual language privileges coordinate systems, light paths, and measured events over decorative space imagery.

### Core Principles
1. **Frames are primary:** Every calculation names the observer’s frame, event coordinates, and what is invariant.
2. **See the geometry:** Light cones, worldlines, and tilted axes appear before symbolic derivation.
3. **Complexity is staged:** Advanced ideas are presented as a chain of small falsifiable claims, not as a sudden wall of equations.
4. **Instrument clarity:** Every control exposes its variable, range, present value, and effect on the model.

### Color Philosophy
Near-black blue is the reading field—an intentional low-glare space for prolonged study. Bone-white carries core text and axes; pale cyan identifies invariant structures such as light; a controlled **event red** identifies a particular event, causal boundary, or conceptual pivot. Muted gold identifies proper quantities, the reference measurements made in the object’s rest frame.

### Layout Paradigm
The module behaves as a **research console with a field journal beneath it**. A compact control rail sets relative speed and events; a large live spacetime viewport owns the primary screen real estate; deliberate reading cards descend in staggered columns to unpack the result.

### Signature Elements
1. **The light cone:** two cyan 45° paths serve as the recurring geometry motif and progress indicator.
2. **Event stamps:** red square markers label an event’s coordinates, observer, and causal class.
3. **Frame tabs:** paired S and S′ indicators are used wherever a comparison is necessary.

### Interaction Philosophy
Learners are asked to alter the observer, not merely adjust a number. As relative velocity changes, transformed event coordinates, Lorentz factor, axes, simultaneity line, and proper-versus-measured quantities update together. States explain their meaning in concise language after the learner changes the instrument.

### Animation
Worldlines interpolate smoothly over 220 ms when a reference-frame parameter changes. The light cone stays stable, visually enforcing invariant c. Event markers move along a single 180 ms transform transition. Equations and results appear with a 10 px upward reveal, no decorative orbital animation. All nonessential motion respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.

### Typography System
**IBM Plex Sans** is the technical voice for headings, variable labels, instrument readouts, and controls; **Newsreader** gives explanatory paragraphs a rigorous but human reading character. Equations render with KaTeX and are always paired with a sentence explaining their role and reference-frame assumptions.

### Brand Essence
**Relativity Observatory makes Special Relativity observable, letting IB Physics students manipulate frames, track events, and reason from invariants.**

**Personality:** exacting, luminous, composed.

### Brand Voice
Headlines identify an operation or a test, never sell an experience. Microcopy names what is invariant, what changes, and whose measurement is being reported.

> “Hold light constant. Let space and time respond.”

> “Switch the observer; the event stays put.”

### Wordmark & Logo
The mark is a **compressed light cone intersected by a single red event square**, paired with an angular “RELATIVITY” wordmark and a smaller “OBSERVATORY” ledger label. The icon must stand alone at favicon size.

### Signature Brand Color
**Event Red — `#E6503E`**. It is reserved for event markers, transformed-state pivots, and causal boundaries.
